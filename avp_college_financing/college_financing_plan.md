## 3. Key Tax & Financial Principles

### 529 Plan Optimization
- **Withdrawals for qualified expenses are 100% federal and CA state tax-free** (earnings portion).
- California conforms to federal 529 treatment; no state deduction for contributions but earnings grow tax-free.
- **Penalty for non-qualified withdrawals:** 10% penalty + ordinary income tax on earnings.
- **Unused 529 funds** can be: (a) transferred between siblings tax-free, (b) rolled into Roth IRA (up to $35,000 lifetime, account must be 15+ years old, subject to annual Roth limits).
- **Priority:** Use 529 funds first for the highest-earnings-ratio dollars to maximize tax savings.

### Treasury Interest (Brokerage Account)
- Treasury interest is **exempt from CA state income tax** (CA marginal rate likely 9.3–12.3%).
- Treasury interest **is subject to federal income tax** at ordinary rates.
- Current T-bill/short-term Treasury yields: ~4.0–4.5% (as of early 2026).
- **After-tax yield estimate:** ~2.8–3.2% (after federal tax at ~32-37% bracket, but no CA tax).

### Federal Student Loans
- **Stafford Unsubsidized:** Interest accrues from disbursement; ~6.5% fixed.
- **Parent PLUS:** ~9.08% fixed; high cost of capital.
- **Student loan interest deduction:** Up to $2,500/yr, but phases out at MAGI >$185,000 (MFJ). At your income level, likely **fully phased out**.
- **FAFSA EFC impact:** High assets will result in high Expected Family Contribution. Cash brokerage counts as parent asset on FAFSA (~5.64% assessment rate). 529 plans owned by parents also assessed at 5.64%.

### Life Insurance Policy Loan
- $400K cash value growing at 8–10% annually; max loan $334K.
- Loan rate: **1% in 2026, 0% from 2027 onward.**
- However, loans reduce the compounding base — at 8–10% growth, every $10K borrowed costs $800–$1,000/yr in lost growth.
- If policy lapses with outstanding loan, the loan becomes taxable income.
- **Reserve as last resort** — preserving this asset's compounding is more valuable than the 0% loan rate suggests.

## 4. Optimal Allocation Strategy

### Core Principle: **Minimize After-Tax Cost of Education**

The optimization hierarchy (best to worst funding source):

| Priority | Source | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 529 Plans | Tax-free growth & withdrawals; best tax alpha |
| 2 | Cash Brokerage (Treasuries) | Already earning ~4%+ pre-tax; CA state tax exempt; liquid |
| 3 | Federal Stafford Loans (student) | Moderate rate; builds student credit; in-school deferment |
| 4 | Life Insurance Loan | 0% rate is great, but reduces death benefit; tax risk on lapse |
| 5 | Parent PLUS Loans | ~9% rate; worst cost of capital — avoid if possible |

### Scenario C: Aggressive Treasury Yield Harvesting — Delay 529, Maximize Interest

**Strategy:** Since Treasury yields (~4.0–4.5%) are state-tax-exempt and you have $1M earning interest, consider front-loading brokerage spending and back-loading 529 withdrawals. This lets the 529 plans continue compounding tax-free while harvesting Treasury income.

**However**, this only makes sense if 529 investment returns > Treasury yields. If the 529 is invested conservatively (similar to Treasuries), there's minimal benefit. If the 529 is in equities/growth, keeping money in 529 longer has higher expected value.

#### Assuming 529 earns ~7% (equity allocation) vs. 4.2% Treasury:

|---|---|---|---|

> *529 growth covers remaining balance; reduced need for Stafford in Year 4.

**Benefit:** Extra ~2.8% compounding on $85,000 for 2 years = ~$4,800 additional tax-free growth per 529 plan. Combined for both children: **~$9,600 additional tax-free savings**.

**Risk:** If 529 equity investments decline, you may need more from brokerage. Sequence-of-returns risk.

**Recommendation:** Only pursue if 529 plans are in growth-oriented funds. If 529s are in conservative/age-based portfolios, use Scenario A.

### Reducing FAFSA Impact (Legal Strategies)
- Paying down mortgage or making retirement contributions reduces reportable assets.
- 529 distributions to pay expenses don't count as student income.
- Two students in college simultaneously splits the parent contribution component (this helps you in 2026-27 through 2029-30 if both overlap).

## 9. Key Takeaways

1. **You do not need PLUS loans or life insurance borrowing** in any realistic scenario. Your $1M brokerage provides more than sufficient runway.

2. **Take Stafford loans anyway** (~$27K per student over 4 years). At 6.5%, this is moderate-cost capital that preserves liquidity and lets more of your brokerage earn 4%+ risk-free. The spread is negative (~2.3% cost), but the optionality and credit-building for your children has value.

3. **Use 529 plans fully** — they represent ~$40,000+ in tax savings across both children. Deplete them before tapping the brokerage beyond what's needed.

4. **Keep Treasuries laddered** to match tuition payment dates. This avoids forced selling at inopportune times and maintains the CA state tax exemption on interest.

5. **The life insurance cash value ($400K at 8–10% growth) is your best-performing asset.** At 0% loan rate (2027+) it looks "free" to borrow, but each $10K loan costs $800–$1,000/yr in lost compounding. Preserve it — by 2034 it could reach $864K–$1.04M untouched.

6. **File FAFSA regardless** — you need it for Stafford eligibility even though need-based aid is unlikely.
